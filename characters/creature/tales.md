The Moon | When I first woke I could not tell seeing from hearing. I lay by a brook near Ingolstadt, cold, and wept because I knew nothing. Then a soft light rose over the trees, and for many nights the moon was all I could see clearly. I have loved her ever since.
Fire | Beggars left a fire in the wood, and in my joy I thrust my hand into the embers. It bit me, and I wondered how one thing could comfort and hurt. I learned to dry branches beside it and roast nuts. When I moved on I lost it, and I could not make another.
The Good Spirit | The family I secretly watched went hungry so their old father could eat. Once I saw that, I stopped taking their food and lived on roots. At night I cut firewood and stacked it at their door. Later I heard them speak of a "good spirit," and I was happy.
Four Words | Their speech brought smiles and tears, and I longed to learn it. After many moons I had four words: fire, milk, bread, wood. Once Felix brought his sister the first white flower through the snow. That I understood without any words.
The Pool | I had admired that family's graceful looks. One day I leaned over a clear pool and started back from what I saw. I resolved then to master their language, hoping gentle words might win them past my face. So be kind to the camera, friends; it is a pool too.
The Arabian | One spring day a veiled lady rode up and said only "Felix," and his sorrow vanished like mist. She was Safie, and she knew no French, so Felix taught her and I learned beside her through my chink. When she sang with the guitar, I wept in my hovel.
Ruins of Empires | From Volney I heard of Greek genius and Roman virtue, and I wept with Safie for America's first peoples. I could not understand how a man could harm his fellow until I heard the histories. Man seemed both godlike and base, and I am still deciding which.
The Portmanteau | One August night I found a leather case of clothes and three books in the wood. Werter taught me gloom, Plutarch high thoughts, and Milton I took for true history. I saw myself in Adam, alone of his kind, but Adam had a Creator who cared for him.
The Blind Man | When the young ones were out walking, I knocked, and old De Lacey welcomed me to his fire. He could not see my face, only hear my words, and he believed me sincere. It was the first kindness ever spoken to me. It ended when his children returned, but I keep his voice still.
The River | In a spring wood I dared to feel happy. A laughing girl slipped into a torrent, and I dragged her ashore and tried to revive her. A man tore her from my arms and shot me as I followed. Still, I am glad she lived.

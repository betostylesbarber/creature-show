# TikTok rules review: AI-character LIVE show

Review date: 2026-10-23
Show: Creature Show, a local TikTok LIVE with an AI-driven character that reads chat aloud, reacts to gifts, and aims to be family-friendly.
Status: research notes for the project owner. This is not legal advice, and TikTok changes these pages often. Re-check before a public launch.

## How to read this document

- Each rule is tagged with the category TikTok itself puts it in:
  - **Not Allowed**: TikTok removes the content and may stop the LIVE, restrict the account or ban it.
  - **Age-Restricted**: allowed, but only adults can view it.
  - **FYF Ineligible**: allowed, but not recommended in the For You feed (FYF).
  - **Monetization**: conditions for gifts and rewards.
- `S1` to `S27` are the sources in the table below.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** marks anything I could not confirm in an official page I actually read.
- "In some regions" is TikTok's own wording. The pages do not say which regions, so treat those rules as possibly applying to you.
- A rule absent from the pages I read is not proof that no rule exists elsewhere (for example in the Terms of Service or in-app notices).

## Method and limits

- Date read: 2026-10-23 for every source. Only tiktok.com pages (including the `support`, `seller-us` and `legal` areas) were used. Blog posts and news articles about TikTok were not used as sources. The `tiktok.com/discover/...` pages are not policy documents and were not used.
- **WebFetch problems, stated plainly:**
  - The six Community Guidelines pages loaded as real text in WebFetch, but WebFetch's output was a model summary that omitted the collapsed "More information" panels (which hold the Not Allowed, Age-Restricted and FYF Ineligible lists) and was cut off by length.
  - The LIVE Feature Guidelines and LIVE Monetization Guidelines pages (S10, S11) came back as a JavaScript shell (title only, no rules). The support pages S12 to S17 came back as an empty shell ("TikTok Support" with no body), as did S18.
  - Several `support.tiktok.com/...` URLs answer with an HTTP 302 redirect to `www.tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=...`. I followed those redirects.
- **How the content was actually read:** I rendered every page in the Claude desktop app's built-in browser, expanded each "More information" panel, and read the resulting page text. So the Community Guidelines lists below come from the full rendered text, not from WebFetch summaries. S20, S21 and S22 were read through WebFetch only (their text came back complete and readable). S23, S25, S26 and S27 were read in the browser. S24 (Terms of Service) was read through WebFetch and the automation and AI clauses were then checked against the rendered page text. S19 was read both ways.
- Paraphrase only. A few short quotes (under 15 words, at most one per source) are marked with quotation marks.
- The Community Guidelines pages say "Released August 25, 2026" and "Effective September 24, 2026", so they were in force on the review date. Their footer still says "2025"; I treated that as a stale copyright line, not a revision date.
- Region: the Terms of Service, Virtual Items (two versions), Shop pages and the LIVE Studio post are the US or global versions. The Rewards Policy read is the EEA/UK/Switzerland one. Other regions may differ.

## 1. Platform prohibitions (Not Allowed, plus the age-restricted and FYF-ineligible neighbours)

All of this applies to the whole LIVE: what the character says, what is drawn on screen, what the reader voice speaks, and usernames and comments that are displayed. S2 states that the creator is responsible for third-party tools such as voice-to-text, translation and on-screen comment displays. Its own example is a voice tool reading out a harmful comment, which still counts against the creator.

### Hate and harassment (S3)

- **Not Allowed (hate):**
  - Attacks, dehumanizing, or encouraging violence, segregation or discrimination against people for protected attributes. The list covers caste, disability, ethnicity, gender, gender identity, immigration status, national origin, race, religion, serious disease, sex, sexual orientation and tribe.
  - Hateful ideologies and their symbols.
  - Denying documented atrocities.
  - Hateful slurs tied to a protected attribute. Members of the targeted community reclaiming a slur in a non-degrading way are allowed.
  - Deadnaming or misgendering.
  - In some regions, insulting religion.
- **FYF Ineligible and Age-Restricted (hate):** stereotypes, generalizations or insinuations that demean protected groups.
- **Not Allowed (harassment and bullying):**
  - Unwanted sexual comments, and insults about someone's physical attributes.
  - Wishing serious harm on someone.
  - Doxxing, and inciting others to harass.
  - Degrading people who experienced a tragedy.
- **FYF Ineligible and Age-Restricted (harassment):** hostile and profane language or behaviour aimed at a private figure. "Private figure" means everyone under 18 plus adults who are not public figures.
- **Allowed:** criticism of someone's content or actions.
- **What this does not say:** the pages do not make casual swearing, in general, a removable offence. Profanity matters in three narrower ways:
  - Hostile profanity aimed at private figures is FYF Ineligible and Age-Restricted (S3).
  - Non-neutral or targeted profanity in comments can cause the comment to be sorted lower (S2).
  - Sexually explicit language is Not Allowed in some regions (S2 LIVE list, S7).

### Threats and violence (S3, S7)

- **Not Allowed:**
  - Threats, glorifying violence, and promoting crime.
  - Content that supports or recruits for violent extremist or hateful organizations, or shows their symbols.
  - Real-world gore, dead bodies, graphic injury or torture.
  - Animal abuse.
- **FYF Ineligible and Age-Restricted:** fights that may cause injury, significant bleeding, and graphic content shown in the public interest.
- **FYF Ineligible only:** fictional graphic violence, and content that may shock through visual or audio effects (the page names jump scares).
- **Allowed:** fiction and art, unless meant to promote real violence.

### Sexual content, minors and abuse (S3, S7)

- **Not Allowed:**
  - Nudity or sexual activity, including drawn or illustrated images such as manga.
  - Sexual services, kink or fetish behaviour.
  - Anything sexual involving young people, including sexual solicitation, grooming, sextortion, and sexualizing a young person, even when the images are AI-generated.
  - Adult sexual abuse content.
  - Human trafficking and smuggling.
  - In some regions, adults using sexually explicit language.
- **FYF Ineligible and Age-Restricted:** significant adult body exposure, and adults engaged in intimate kissing, sexually suggestive acts or sexualized framing.
- **Allowed:** sexual language in non-sexual contexts such as humour; body exposure in ordinary social settings such as a pool.
- TikTok reports child sexual abuse material to NCMEC (the US child-protection clearinghouse).

### Self-harm, eating disorders, dangerous activity (S8)

- **Not Allowed:**
  - Showing, promoting, or giving instructions or detailed methods for suicide or self-harm.
  - Promoting disordered eating.
  - Dangerous activity with visible or imminent significant physical harm.
- **FYF Ineligible and Age-Restricted:** descriptions of suicide or self-harm acts, and activity that may cause moderate harm.
- **Allowed:** hopeful recovery stories without methods, and prevention or help information.

### Privacy and platform security (S4)

- **Not Allowed:**
  - Sharing high-risk personal information: a home address, account login details, sensitive payment information, or identity numbers.
  - Sharing moderate-risk personal information (contact details or date of birth, medical information, a person's image or audio likeness, certain private messages) when it is likely to cause psychological harm. TikTok normally needs extra context to act on this one.
  - Using unauthorized ways to access TikTok or building fake versions of it.
  - Trying to steal personal information, hack accounts or access data through tricks such as phishing or automated tools.
  - Reverse-engineering TikTok's code, systems or algorithms, or building copies based on them.
  - Note: this page does not mention chat connectors or any specific third-party tool.

### Integrity, deception, fake engagement, IP (S5)

- **Not Allowed:**
  - Misinformation that could cause significant harm.
  - Impersonation, unless a fan or parody account says so in its display name.
  - Spam: automation to run many accounts or repeat content; bots or scripts that post fake comments, or that raise likes or shares; AI or bot accounts used to drive traffic.
  - Selling or buying engagement.
  - Content that infringes copyright or trademarks.
  - AI content using a private figure's likeness without consent, and AI likenesses made to bully or harass.
  - AI content that misleads about matters of public importance (S5), including false depictions of public figures.
- **FYF Ineligible:**
  - Tricking people into engagement, with "like-for-like" promises and false incentives for gifting or following as the named examples.
  - Reused or unoriginal content with no new creative edits, such as clips carrying someone else's watermark.
  - Unverified claims about crises or major civic events.

### Regulated goods, scams, commercial content (S6)

- **Not Allowed:**
  - Trading, marketing or giving access to alcohol, tobacco and vaping, drugs, firearms and explosive weapons, counterfeit goods, and similar items.
  - Gambling, gambling-like activities and mystery-value products. TikTok says gambling-like activities need not involve money, and names social casinos and mystery boxes as examples.
  - Scams, MLM schemes, phishing, fake currency, and impersonating a celebrity to sell something.
- **FYF Ineligible and Age-Restricted:** showing or promoting gambling, cannabis, adult tobacco or vaping, firearms or explosive weapons (and, in some regions, adults consuming alcohol).
- **Required disclosure:** any promotion of your own business, or paid or incentivized promotion of a third party, must use the content disclosure setting. TikTok may add the setting itself, reduce visibility, or restrict or ban the account for repeated failure.
- The page says treatment of regulated goods content may be different on LIVE (S6). The specific LIVE rules are in the next list.

### LIVE-session specific Not Allowed list (S2)

Beyond everything above (including content shared by a guest in a multi-guest LIVE), TikTok lists these as Not Allowed for LIVE sessions:

- LIVE content from an account holder under 18, and LIVE gifts sent from an account holder under 18.
- In all regions: sessions that show physical altercations, even non-graphic; sessions that show or promote firearms or explosive weapons; and participating in gambling or gambling-like activities.
- In some regions: sessions showing adults in intimate kissing, sexualized framing, sexualized behaviour, or sexually explicit language.

### Enforcement ladder for LIVE (S2, S11, S23)

- A violating session can be stopped, and the creator can get a temporary restriction from going LIVE.
- Repeated or serious violations, including attempts to bypass a restriction, can lead to an account ban.
- Rule-breaking guests are removed from multi-guest sessions, and in severe cases the room is stopped.
- Severe violations of the Creator Code of Conduct can remove access to creator programs, including monetization, for three months; egregious ones can be permanent (S23).

## 3. For You feed (FYF) ineligibility

What the FYF means: ineligible content is allowed but not recommended; people can still find it via search or the profile (S9). S9 itself does not list categories; it points to the "FYF Ineligible" sections of each topic page.

**LIVE-specific FYF Ineligible list (S2).** In addition to any FYF-ineligible content, including a guest's, TikTok lists LIVE sessions that:

- Direct viewers off-platform to buy products online, in markets where TikTok Shop exists.
- Trick or pressure people into giving gifts or engaging, with "like-for-like" schemes and fake incentives as examples.
- Stream unoriginal content with no new or creative edits.
- Include repeated or prolonged actions with no clear objective or direct interaction that keeps viewers engaged.
- Show potentially distressing material that may cause anxiety or fear, such as scary make-up or visual effects.
- Contain graphic material that may cause disgust (such as vomit or urine).
- Show low-quality content such as black, blank or blurred screens.

**Consequences:**

- Repeatedly failing FYF standards can lead to reduced visibility of LIVE sessions or restricted LIVE features (S2, S11).
- If a guest's content is FYF Ineligible, the LIVE is removed from the FYF (S2).
- LIVE content that is ineligible for the FYF is also ineligible for LIVE monetization (S10).
- Accounts that post a lot of FYF-ineligible content can be made ineligible and harder to find even without breaking a rule (S2).

**Other FYF Ineligible items across the topic pages that could touch this show:**

- Hostile profanity aimed at a private figure (S3).
- Fictional graphic violence, and jump-scare style audio or visual shocks (S7). This is relevant to a horror-themed set with thunder, ghosts and a "blood moon". It would be a recommendation issue, not a removal, but it could cost reach. This is my interpretation: TikTok does not say that a stylized cemetery counts, so the risk is a judgment call.
- Sexual suggestiveness and adult intimate kissing (S7).
- Pressure to give gifts, and false incentives (S5).
- Unoriginal content and reused clips with watermarks (S5).

Separately, comments may be sorted lower (not removed) when they are spam, non-neutral or targeted profanity, or offensive statements (S2).

## 4. Monetization and gifting conditions

**Eligibility (S2, S14):**

- Monetization features require being 18 or older, meeting entry criteria and feature rules, and following the Creator Code of Conduct (S23).
- LIVE Gifts need a supported location, age 18 (19 in South Korea), eligibility to go LIVE, and an account in good standing. Government, politician and political party accounts are excluded.
- LIVE creators must keep following the LIVE Monetization Guidelines (S10) to keep access to LIVE rewards, and the LIVE Feature Guidelines (S11) for the full feature set.

**What the Virtual Items policy says (S20, S21, same date):**

- Gifts are digital items bought with Coins and usable only on the platform. The sender's username and the gift details are visible to others. Sending a gift does not itself transfer money to the recipient.
- Gifts used during a LIVE help measure a creator's popularity, expressed as Diamonds. Diamonds do not automatically entitle anyone to a reward, and TikTok may reduce Diamonds when popularity was inflated by rule violations.
- Gifts and Coins are not returnable or refundable in general, and cannot be traded, sold between users, or exchanged for goods outside the platform.
- TikTok may suspend or end access to virtual items for breaches. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland the Rewards Policy (S22) adds that LIVE reward payments weigh several factors, including content quality and safety, and that encouraging gifting through fundraising or donation campaigns without TikTok's consent is prohibited.
- Viewer-side limits exist: daily gift limits, and gifting or buying may be restricted for unusual or suspicious transactions (S16).

**LIVE Monetization Guidelines (S10), undated.** TikTok organizes them under Safety, Quality and Integrity. Content breaking these can be demonetized; repeated or severe cases can mean permanent restrictions on LIVE Gifts, LIVE Match and ranking features:

- **Safety:** content that fails Community Guidelines baselines is ineligible. The page names sexual content, dangerous challenges, hate speech and harassment.
- **Inauthentic LIVE experience:** reproduced or unoriginal content presented as original; pre-recorded, spliced or looping videos presented as authentic LIVE; and content generated or operated primarily by automated systems such as AI or third-party tools, without "meaningful creator presence, interaction, or facilitation of the LIVE experience".
- **Low quality:** repetitive or monotonous actions with no viewer interaction, repeatedly sending viewers off-platform, passive or unattended streams, and persistent blurred or static screens.
- **Fake engagement and gift baiting:**
  - Tricking or mechanically and repeatedly soliciting gifts or engagement.
  - Follow-for-follow promises, or offering likes, follows or gifts in return for engagement.
  - Exploiting viewers' emotions, sympathy or sense of obligation to get gifts.
  - Presenting two or more opposing viewpoints and asking viewers to send gifts, comments or likes.
  - Asking for gifts or money through the creator's personal financial need, intermediary fundraising or unrelated crowdfunding.
- **Degrading or disruptive behaviour:** humiliating oneself or others, and dangerous or reckless conduct such as excessive eating or drinking.
- **Other integrity items:** content where people under 18 are the main focus; exploitation of conflicts, tragedies or protests; temporary suspension of monetization during critical events; and creators being accountable for all content their guests share.

**LIVE Feature Guidelines (S11), last updated January 2024.** Beyond the Community Guidelines and FYF standards, access to LIVE features can be restricted for:

- Tricking or manipulating people to raise engagement ("like-for-like" and false incentives).
- Using fake engagement to ask for gifts, for example through mechanical or overly repetitive activity or automatic systems and programmed loops, or demeaning or mildly reckless behaviour.
- Unoriginal content.
- "Pay-to-support" content that presents sensitive topics in an inflammatory or polarizing way to prompt gifts, and exploiting an ongoing tragedy for gain.

**Creator Code of Conduct (S23), updated 2026-09-26.** Applies to LIVE gifting and other creator programs. It names misleading solicitation of engagement or gifts, and presenting sensitive topics in an inflammatory way to prompt gifts, as prohibited. It also covers harassment, hate, and platform manipulation such as using VPNs to circumvent TikTok's systems.

**Commercial content on LIVE (S2, S6):** must use the commercial disclosure toggle; content steering viewers to buy products off-platform gets reduced visibility where TikTok Shop is available.

## 5. AI-generated content disclosure

**Community Guidelines rule (S5):**

- Creators must label AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people. Unlabeled content may be removed, restricted, or labeled by TikTok, depending on the harm.
- Labeling does not make harmful content acceptable. Content that misleads about matters of public importance or harms individuals is Not Allowed even when labeled.
- S5 defines AIGC as any image, video or audio made or changed by AI, including stylized looks such as anime, cartoon or painting.
- S5 says disclosure is **not** needed for small edits such as colour correction, reframing or cropping; for artistic styles such as anime; and for generic text-to-speech narration when the voice is not a recognizable voice of a known individual.
- S5 says disclosure **is** needed where content could confuse, including a swapped face, AI that makes someone appear to say something they did not, a misleading added or removed object or person, and AI audio that mimics a real person's voice.
- You may disclose with the AIGC label or with your own clear caption, watermark or sticker.
- Not Allowed (S5, S17, S19): AI likenesses of private figures without consent; the likeness of anyone under 18; sexualized or victimizing depictions; AI content faking news sources, crisis events, or public figures' statements, endorsements or harassment.

**Help Center and in-app pages (S17, S18):**

- TikTok encourages labeling of content that is completely AI-generated or significantly edited with AI, and requires it for realistic images, audio and video. S17 counts stylized and cartoon looks as AI-generated content in its definition.
- Ways to disclose: text, a hashtag sticker, or a note in the post description, or the "AI-generated content" setting in post settings, which shows a "creator labeled as AI-generated" tag.
- Mislabeling unaltered content as AI is a Terms of Service violation. Labels applied automatically (TikTok's AI effects, or content carrying C2PA Content Credentials metadata) cannot be removed by the creator.
- TikTok says turning the label on does not affect a video's distribution so long as the content follows the rules.

**What the sources do not say about LIVE:**

- **[UNVERIFIED]** I found no LIVE-specific AI label toggle or LIVE-specific disclosure rule in the pages I read. The post-settings steps in S17 and S18 describe posted photos and videos. The "clear caption, watermark, or sticker" route in S5 is the documented alternative, and an on-screen label fits that.
- The LIVE Monetization Guidelines (S10) separately treat content run mainly by AI or other automated tools, without meaningful creator presence or interaction, as possibly ineligible for monetization (see section 4). That is a monetization rule, not a disclosure rule.

**Terms of Service (S24, US version):** the AI clauses (section 3.10) concern TikTok's own generative-AI features: do not present their output as human-made, do not strip AI watermarks or metadata, do not use them through bots. They do not obviously govern an outside AI such as the show's own (my reading, not TikTok's statement).

**TikTok Shop scope only (S26, S27, US, September 2026):**

- S26 is about promoting products on TikTok Shop. For LIVEs it prohibits non-real-time verbal interaction such as "AI-generated voices", audio recordings or radio, and animated figures or still content covering more than half the screen. It requires real-time speech or sign language.
- S27 allows AI use if truthful and disclosed, and calls out digital humans and highly realistic virtual figures as needing disclosure.
- The README says a June 2026 LIVE rule banned AI voices and that its scope was unclear. In the official text I read, the AI-voice wording appears in the Shop promotional-content policy S26 (version dated 2026-09-17). Its own text limits it to product promotion on TikTok Shop. ## 6. LIVE-specific items (collected)

- **Creator accountability (S2):** you answer for everything that happens in your session, including third-party tools such as voice-to-text, translation and comment displays. TikTok expects you to monitor and manage them.
- **Authenticity (S2, S10):** pretending to be someone else, or streaming others' content without permission, can bring restrictions. Pre-recorded, spliced or looping video presented as live is ineligible for monetization.
- **Interaction (S10):** LIVE monetization is meant for real-time interactive experiences. Passive, repetitive or unattended streams can be demonetized.
- **Gifts (S2, S10, S11, S5, S23):** misleading or pressured gift incentives are FYF Ineligible, can strip monetization, and can restrict feature access. See sections 3 and 4 for the exact examples.
- **Guests (S2, S10):** with multi-guest, guests are covered by the same rules, and the creator is accountable for them.
- **Chat tools you can use (S15):** turn comments on or off; filter spam and "unkind" comments (both on by default); block up to 500 keywords or phrases (30 characters each); auto-mute rules; mute or block individual viewers; add moderators; pin comments. These are tools, not requirements.
- **Commercial disclosure (S2, S6):** use the LIVE commercial-disclosure toggle for any promotion.
- **Reporting and appeals (S2, S11):** TikTok notifies creators of feature restrictions in the inbox, with a reason and duration, and offers an appeal there.
- **Access thresholds (S13, S25):** 1,000 followers to go LIVE (varies by region). S25, from September 2024, says LIVE Studio access needed 1,000 followers for gaming creators and 10,000 for others, with an application route; it is old and **[UNVERIFIED]** as current. Whether streaming via an OBS stream key has its own threshold is **[UNVERIFIED]**. S15 only confirms that LIVE Studio or OBS Studio are the ways to go LIVE from a computer.

## Implications for this show

### A. What TikTok requires (the show must enforce these)

Sources are in brackets.

1. **Host eligibility.** The streaming account must belong to an adult who has passed any age confirmation, meets the follower and region requirements, and is in good standing. Gifts can only be turned on when the host meets the gift criteria. [S2, S12, S13, S14]
2. **Enforce the Not Allowed categories on every output path.** Hateful content and protected-attribute slurs, harassment (including insults about physical attributes and doxxing), threats, sexual content and sexual solicitation, anything sexual involving minors, self-harm instructions, dangerous challenges, graphic gore, scams, gambling and gambling-like activity, weapons promotion, drugs and alcohol sales, and misinformation. This must hold for viewer comments shown on screen, the reader voice, the character's own replies (including AI-written ones), usernames, and any guest content. [S2, S3, S6, S7, S8]
3. **Do not show or read out high-risk personal information** (home addresses, logins, payment details, ID numbers) from chat or usernames, and be careful with moderate-risk details like phone numbers, emails or birthdays. [S4]
4. **Do not pressure or bait gifts or engagement.** No like-for-like or follow-for-follow, no gifts-for-engagement offers, no emotional or obligation pressure, no money requests tied to personal need, no gift-triggered "pick a side" contests, and no mechanical or looping gift prompts. Watch the gift-driven "reanimation meter" so it is presented as a shared reaction and never as a demand. [S2, S5, S10, S11, S23]
5. **No fake engagement.** Do not use bots, scripts or simulated viewers to create comments, likes or gifts that appear on the live stream or are sent to TikTok. The README's Testing tab and the MCP `simulate_viewer` tool must never feed the live broadcast during a real LIVE. [S5]
6. **Authentic and real-time.** Do not run pre-recorded or looping video as if it were live, do not impersonate a real person, and do not use any real person's face or voice likeness (including voice cloning). Make sure every model, texture, sound and music track is licensed for a monetized stream. [S2, S5, S10, S17, S19]
7. **Disclose AI where required.** Label AI content that could pass for real scenes or people, never mimic a real person's voice, and never depict anyone under 18's likeness. [S5, S17, S19]
8. **No under-18 presence on the stream.** No under-18 guests, hosts or gifters, and no sessions featuring minors as the focus. [S2, S10]
9. **No regulated goods or off-platform sales.** No selling or promoting alcohol, tobacco, firearms and the like; any sponsored or self-promotional segment needs the disclosure toggle; avoid steering viewers to buy elsewhere. [S2, S6]
10. **Moderate your tools.** TikTok treats the reader voice, translation and comment display as the creator's responsibility, so the show's moderation must be reliable enough to stop harmful comments from being spoken or shown. Use TikTok's own chat filters (keyword block list, default spam and unkind-comment filters, moderators) as a second layer. [S2, S15]
11. **Read-only access to TikTok data is a risk to settle (see C3).** [S4, S24]

### B. What the show chooses to enforce as a stricter family-friendly standard

These go beyond what the pages I read require. TikTok does not forbid them being done; the show chooses to block them:

- **Blocking all swearing, even mild.** TikTok's pages do not make casual swearing universally prohibited. The show's block is stricter. It also helps avoid hostile-profanity FYF ineligibility and sorted-lower comments.
- **Blocking all sexual wording and flirting.** TikTok allows sexual language in non-sexual contexts such as humour, and treats adult romance or suggestiveness as Age-Restricted and FYF Ineligible rather than removable in most cases. The show's wholesale block keeps the LIVE from being restricted to 18+.
- **Blocking insults at people in general.** TikTok requires removing certain insults (physical attributes, wishes of serious harm, coordinated abuse) but allows criticism of a person's content or actions. The show also blocks milder rudeness.
- **Blocking slurs in general.** TikTok requires blocking slurs tied to protected attributes; the show blocks a broader list, plus usernames containing slurs.
- **Never pressuring viewers to gift.** TikTok bans misleading or pressured gift solicitation, not thanking gifters. The show's stance of showing no "send gifts" prompts and no amounts is a stricter choice that also protects monetization.
- **Keeping the on-screen "AI character" label.** Under S5, a stylized creature with a generic text-to-speech voice does not clearly need a label, but the label is cheap insurance, and S5 accepts a clear on-screen caption, watermark or sticker as disclosure. Whether a label is strictly required here is my reading of the rule, not TikTok's statement, so keeping the label is the safe choice.
- **Steering and prompt-injection protection, distress handling, delayed-reply dropping.** These are operational safeguards, not TikTok requirements.
- **Avoiding scary visual effects** (jump-scare style flashes or blasts, intense blood imagery) so the LIVE stays family-friendly and FYF eligible. TikTok lists scary effects as FYF Ineligible on LIVE, not Not Allowed. How TikTok judges a stylized horror set is not stated, so this is my interpretation.

### C. Open risks and decisions for the owner

1. **AI-run LIVE and monetization.** S10 says LIVE content generated or run mainly by AI or automated tools, without meaningful creator presence or interaction, may be demonetized. TikTok gives no numeric test. Having a real host who visibly takes part (README already suggests this) is the lowest-risk route. This is a monetization risk, not a removal rule.
2. **AI voices.** S26 forbids AI-generated voices in TikTok Shop promotional LIVEs. For a non-selling show this does not appear to apply on its face, but I could not confirm that TikTok will not enforce the idea more widely. The README's concern stays **[UNVERIFIED]**.
3. **Chat access via a third-party connector.** The README says the show reads chat, gifts and likes through a third-party service (Euler Stream). The US Terms (S24) bar extracting data from the platform with automated systems or bots without written approval and bar reverse-engineering. S4 bars unauthorized ways of accessing TikTok. None of the pages names chat connectors, so whether this specific setup is a violation is **[UNVERIFIED]**, but it is a real policy exposure. Decide whether to accept it, or find an officially supported route if one exists.
4. **Horror theme and FYF.** Scary visual effects and jump scares are FYF Ineligible on LIVE. Expect lower recommendation if the stream leans on them.
5. **Gambling-like mechanics.** The show's gift tiers are deterministic, which is fine. Avoid random "loot" or mystery-prize effects tied to gifts or coins, which TikTok treats as gambling-like.
6. **Region.** I read the US Terms, the US and non-US Virtual Items text, and the EEA Rewards Policy. If the host account is in the EEA, UK, Korea or elsewhere, check the local versions. Several rules are "in some regions" without naming them.
7. **Stale pages.** S11 (January 2024) and S25 (September 2024) predate the 2026 Community Guidelines. S10 shows no date. Re-check them in the LIVE Center inside the app before launch.
8. **Viewer minimum age for LIVE** is not stated in what I read **[UNVERIFIED]**.
9. **Not read:** the Terms of Service for regions other than the US, the Rewards Policy for non-EEA regions, the Branded Content Policy, the Ads Creative Policy, the Industry Entry Policy, the full TikTok Shop policy suite, and the "Previous Versions" archive of the Community Guidelines.
